## Where Tenant sits competitively and the 3 highest-leverage gaps

Tenant is still third for a power user, but it is no longer a generation behind on the basics. It closed the two gaps I ranked first last time in their core form: commands can run in the background and files can be paged, and the model can see images. Those two dimensions carry 22 of my 100 weight points and account for most of the gain. It remains the best engineered and the most careful of the three, and its local-model handling is now within a point of the leaders.

What did not move is everything about reach and autonomy depth. Channels, extensibility, autonomy, multi-agent and the learning loop are each unchanged, and together they are 42 weight points on which Tenant trails by two to six points per dimension. Both rivals kept adding in exactly those areas over the same six weeks.

The three highest-leverage gaps, by weight times distance to the leader:

1. **Reach: channels and a skills ecosystem.** Channels (4 vs 10) is now the largest single weighted deficit, and extensibility (6 vs 9) is next to it. Add Telegram, Slack and WhatsApp, run the scripts that skills bundle, and give users a way to install community skills. Nothing here started on main this period.
2. **Acting on what it sees: vision-driven browsing and computer use.** Vision input landed, but the browser cannot click at a coordinate and there is no desktop control. Add coordinate actions tied to the screenshot, full-page views, and a desktop `computer` tool. This is the remaining four points on a 10-weight dimension.
3. **Execution and autonomy depth.** Add a PTY and a persistent code runner, and let background jobs run inside the sandbox (tool execution, 6 vs 9, is still the heaviest dimension). Alongside that, add event triggers (webhook, heartbeat) with delivery beyond Discord, and a worktree or sandbox per sub-agent. The learning loop also had no change at all and is now three points behind both rivals.
